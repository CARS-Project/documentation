2  Who holds vehicle data in the UK

Information about vehicles in the UK is collected by several government bodies, each for its own administrative purpose. Knowing who collects what, and why, explains much of what is in the CARS data and what is missing from it.

2.1 The Department for Transport and its agencies

The Department for Transport (DfT) is responsible for transport policy. Two of its executive agencies hold the records that CARS draws on, and DfT itself publishes official statistics built from them.

Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA)

The DVLA, based in Swansea, keeps the register of vehicles (and of drivers) for Great Britain, and since 2014 for Northern Ireland as well. Every vehicle used on public roads must be registered. The register records the vehicle’s identity and specification, its registered keeper, and its tax status. The DVLA:

  • issues the registration number (Chapter 5) and the V5C registration certificate (“logbook”), reissued whenever the keeper or a key detail changes;
  • collects vehicle excise duty (vehicle tax), whose rates depend on CO2 emissions, fuel and age, which is why the register records these;
  • records vehicles declared off the road (SORN), exported, or scrapped (Chapter 6).

The keeper’s name and address are personal data and are not published. The DVLA publishes the vehicle details for any registration number through the Vehicle Enquiry Service (VES), used by the public to check a vehicle’s tax and MOT status. CARS uses the VES programming interface (API) to add DVLA details to each vehicle (Chapter 8).

Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA)

The DVSA sets and enforces vehicle testing standards in Great Britain. It runs the MOT scheme (Chapter 1) and the annual test for heavy vehicles, and holds the record of every MOT test. It publishes MOT data in two ways that CARS uses:

  • the anonymised MOT test data: every test, with the test date, result, odometer reading, test class and postcode area of the testing station, but without the registration number;
  • the MOT history API, including a bulk download: every registered vehicle, with its registration number, make, model, fuel and dates, and the history of its MOT tests (but no location).

Driver & Vehicle Agency, Northern Ireland (DVA)

Vehicle testing in Northern Ireland is run separately, by the DVA. Its tests reach the DVSA datasets, but Northern Ireland differs from Great Britain in ways that show in the data: the first test is at four years, tests are carried out at government test centres, and the test class is not supplied (Chapter 1).

2.2 Official statistics

DfT publishes several statistical series about vehicles and their use. They are the natural benchmarks for the CARS data, and CARS uses some of them directly.

Series What it covers How it relates to CARS
Vehicle licensing statistics Counts of licensed and SORN vehicles from the DVLA register, by body type, make, model, fuel, age and area (the VEH tables) CARS uses DfT’s make and model names (VEH0220, VEH0124) and compares its vehicle counts with DfT’s (Chapter 13)
Road traffic statistics Vehicle miles travelled on roads, estimated from traffic counts Measures traffic on the roads of an area, whoever drives it; CARS measures the distance driven by vehicles tested in an area, wherever it is driven
National Travel Survey (NTS) A household survey of how people travel in England, including car mileage Survey-based and England only, but links travel to people and households
Anonymised MOT data Test-level MOT results, published since 2010 One of the CARS sources (Chapter 8)

Other sources often used alongside vehicle data:

  • The Census (ONS, NRS and NISRA) records the number of cars or vans available to each household, for small areas.
  • SMMT (the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders) publishes new vehicle registrations by make, model and fuel.

2.3 Open data and the Open Government Licence

The three sources CARS combines are published under the Open Government Licence (OGL). The OGL allows anyone to copy, adapt, combine and republish the information, including commercially, provided the source is acknowledged. Because each source may be used and republished, so may the combination, and the CARS data products are released on the same basis (Chapter 10).

The difficulty with these sources has never been access. It is that they are very large, structured for administration rather than analysis, and not linked to each other. The bulk MOT history download alone is over 400 GB of compressed JSON files. CARS does the linking and restructuring so that others do not have to.
