5  Number plates

Every vehicle registered in the UK has a vehicle registration mark (VRM), usually called its registration number or number plate (in other countries a licence plate). The VRM is how the CARS sources are joined, and its format says when and, for recent vehicles, where the vehicle was first registered.

The rules for displaying plates are on GOV.UK.

5.1 Formats

Great Britain has used four main formats. Northern Ireland has its own.

Format Years Example What it tells you
Current September 2001 onwards AB12 CDE The first two letters are the memory tag: the first letter is the region and the second the DVLA office. The two digits are the age identifier: 12 for vehicles registered March–August 2012, 62 for September 2012–February 2013. The last three letters are random
Prefix August 1983 to August 2001 A123 BCD The first letter gives the year: A for August 1983–July 1984 and so on, changing every six months from 1999 (T, V, W, X, Y)
Suffix 1963 to July 1983 ABC 123D The last letter gives the year, from A in 1963
Dateless Before 1963 ABC 123 No date
Northern Ireland All years ABZ 1234 Three letters, always including I or Z, and up to four digits. No date

There are also Q plates, for vehicles whose age or identity cannot be established (for example kit cars or rebuilt vehicles), and formats for diplomatic vehicles, the Isle of Man, the Channel Islands and foreign registrations.

5.2 Personalised plates

Registration numbers can be bought and transferred between vehicles, and cherished or personalised plates are popular. A vehicle can carry a plate that makes it look older than it is, but never younger: a 2015 car can carry a 1990 H plate, but not a 2020 20 plate. Dateless and Northern Ireland plates are popular for this, because they hide the vehicle’s age altogether.

Transfers affect the data in two ways:

  • The plate’s date and the vehicle’s age disagree. CARS decodes each plate and flags vanity_plate when the vehicle’s birth date falls outside the plate’s issue period. The flag catches any mismatch, not only personalised plates: imported vehicles, Q plates and re-registered vehicles are flagged too.
  • A registration number can belong to different vehicles at different times. When a plate moves, a lookup by registration number returns the vehicle that carries it now. This is the main reason a DVLA record can describe a different vehicle from the DVSA record it was matched to (Chapter 9).

5.3 Plates in the CARS data

Column Meaning
registration_DVSA The registration as recorded by DVSA, the key for joining to other data
registration_DVLA The registration returned by the DVLA Vehicle Enquiry Service
plate_type The plate format
plate_date, plate_date_from, plate_date_to The issue period the plate implies
plate_location The issuing office or region implied by the memory tag (current format only)
vanity_plate The vehicle’s birth date falls outside the plate’s issue period
ImportantPitfall: plate location is not where the vehicle is

plate_location is the DVLA office the plate was allocated to, and for a new car is usually the region of the dealer that first registered it. It says nothing about where the vehicle is kept now. Use the postcode area of testing (pca_YYYY) for location.
