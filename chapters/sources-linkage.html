8  Source data and linkage

The CARS data is built in two stages. The University of Bristol team links the three source datasets and turns each vehicle’s test history into annual figures. The University of Leeds pipeline then cleans and enhances the result and produces the published tables (Chapter 9). This chapter describes the sources and the Bristol stage.

NoteTo be written: Bristol method

This chapter summarises the Bristol stage from project notes. A full description, by the Bristol team, of the data processing and the database built to carry it out will be added; it is also the subject of the project’s second webinar, Under the bonnet (January 2027).

8.1 The three sources

All three are published under the Open Government Licence (Chapter 2).

Anonymised MOT test data

Every MOT test since 2005: the date, result, odometer reading, test class and the postcode area of the testing station, with the vehicle’s make, model, fuel, engine size and first-use date. Vehicles carry an anonymous identifier rather than their registration number. The data was first published by DfT in 2010 and is now published by DVSA, roughly annually; in recent years over 40 million tests a year are added.

Gives CARS: the location of each test, and the test class.

DVSA MOT history (bulk download)

Every vehicle registered in Great Britain, including those not yet tested, with its registration number, make, model, fuel, engine size, colour, dates of first use, registration and manufacture, and its full MOT history: every test with its date, result and odometer reading. There is no location. The download is available through the MOT history API to anyone who applies for a key, and is a set of zipped JSON files of over 400 GB.

Gives CARS: the registration number, which links to the DVLA, and the backbone of one record per vehicle.

DVLA Vehicle Enquiry Service

The DVLA’s Vehicle Enquiry Service API returns, for one registration number at a time, the vehicle’s details from the DVLA register: make, colour, fuel, engine capacity, CO2 emissions, Euro status, type approval category, revenue weight, wheel plan, tax status and due date, MOT status and expiry, year of manufacture, month of first registration, whether it is marked for export, and the date of the latest V5C.

Gives CARS: the emissions and specification details that DVSA does not hold, and evidence of whether and when the vehicle was taxed, declared SORN or exported.

8.2 Linking the anonymised MOT data to the MOT history

The anonymised test data has the location but no registration number; the MOT history has the registration number but no location. The two are linked through the tests themselves: the sequence of test dates and odometer readings of a vehicle is, in practice, a unique fingerprint. Matching fingerprints attaches the postcode area of each test to the vehicle in the MOT history.

The fingerprint matching method: which fields are used, how ties and partial matches are resolved, and the match rate.

8.3 Linking the DVLA records

DVLA details were collected by querying the Vehicle Enquiry Service for every registration number in the DVSA download, many of them several times between 2024 and 2026. Each query returns the vehicle that carries that registration at the time of the query. Because registrations can move between vehicles (Chapter 5), a query does not always describe the same vehicle as the DVSA record with that registration, and the Bristol team built rules to decide which DVSA vehicle each returned record belongs to.

The Leeds pipeline adds a further check, comparing the make, year and engine size of the DVSA and DVLA records, and does not use DVLA values where they clearly describe a different vehicle (Chapter 9).

NoteTo be written

A description of the VES linking rules to accompany the flowchart, and of the VES status categories: data returned; invalid registration that will never return data; invalid registration with a proposed cleaned version that does; vehicle not found, assumed to have left the register long ago; vehicle not found now but found at some point; never queried.

8.4 Annualisation

The tests of a vehicle happen on whatever dates they happen. For analysis it is far more useful to have one figure per vehicle per calendar year. The Bristol team’s annualisation converts each vehicle’s test history into, for each year from 2005 to 2025:

  • mileage_YYYY: the distance driven in the year, in miles;
  • legit_mileage_YYYY: whether that mileage is plausible;
  • pca_YYYY: the postcode area the vehicle is assigned to in the year;
  • flags describing the vehicle’s stage of life in the year (Chapter 6).

Annual mileage

The odometer readings at two consecutive tests give the distance driven between them. That distance is shared across the calendar years the interval spans, however long the interval, so a year can only have a mileage if it is covered by an interval with an odometer reading at each end.

NoteTo be confirmed

How the distance is shared between years (we understand it to be in proportion to the time in each, so an interval from March 2018 to March 2019 contributes most of its distance to 2018), and how the distance before the first test is treated.

Plausible mileage

Odometer readings contain errors: typing mistakes, odometers replaced or reset, readings in kilometres taken as miles, and occasional fraud (clocking). The Bristol team judges whether each annual mileage is plausible, and removes the mileage where it is not. legit_mileage_YYYY therefore has three states: TRUE (plausible), blank with a mileage (not assessed) and FALSE (judged implausible; the mileage is then blank).

NoteTo be written

The rules for judging mileage plausible, including the thresholds on miles per day, the treatment of odometer units and resets, and what share of readings each rule removes.

Postcode area in each year

A vehicle is assigned the postcode area of the test closest in time to the year for which a postcode area is available. Years with no test nearby have no postcode area, and the Leeds pipeline then labels them unborn, unknown or dead (Chapter 6).

NoteTo be written

The exact rule for choosing the test that gives a year its postcode area, and how many years it can reach across.

8.5 What the Bristol stage produces

The output of the Bristol stage is one table with one row per vehicle, 138,238,617 rows in the delivery the current data is built from, and around 200 columns: the DVSA vehicle details, the per-year columns, the attached DVLA columns, and columns recording the history of the DVLA lookups. It is the input to the pipeline described in Chapter 9. It is not itself published (Chapter 10).
