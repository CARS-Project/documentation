10  Data products and access

CARS releases its data as a family of products at different levels of detail. Choose the least detailed product that answers your question: the aggregate tables are much easier to work with, and have already dealt with several of the pitfalls described in Chapter 14.

Product Unit Format Size Best for
Aggregate tables Postcode area × year × vehicle characteristics CSV KB to a few MB each Spreadsheet users; maps; trends; comparisons between areas
Vehicle table One row per vehicle, with one column per year Parquet about 9 GB Custom groupings; cohorts; individual makes and models; anything the tables do not cover
Reports and quality tables Documentation Markdown, CSV Small Understanding how each table was made and how complete it is

10.1 Aggregate tables

The aggregate tables count vehicles and sum their mileage by postcode area and year, broken down by body type, fuel, age, engine size and other characteristics. There are three families, described in Chapter 12 and catalogued in Appendix B:

  • mileage tables: vehicles and annual mileage by postcode area, year, body type and fuel;
  • fleet profile tables: the make-up of the fleet by year, body type and age band;
  • churn tables: how the vehicles in each postcode area change from year to year as vehicles arrive, move, age and leave.

Vehicles that cannot be located in a year are included in the tables under the statuses unknown, dead and unborn, so totals can be reconciled.

10.2 The vehicle table

vehicles_enhanced.parquet has one row for each of the 93.4 million vehicles in scope, and 166 columns, including 21 years each of mileage, plausibility flag and postcode area. It is described in Chapter 11, and every column is listed in the data dictionary.

10.3 The linked source table

The Bristol team’s linked and annualised table (Chapter 8), from which the vehicle table is built, holds every vehicle in the DVSA download, including those that left the roads before 2005.

10.4 Licensing and conditions of use

The sources are published under the Open Government Licence v3.0, which permits combining and republishing them, and the CARS products are released on the same basis.

NoteTo be written

The licence of each product, the attribution statement users must include (“Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0”), and any conditions of use.

Registration numbers and personal data

The vehicle table includes each vehicle’s registration number, because it is the key for linking to other data. A registration number is not in itself personal information, and all the sources are published openly. However, a registration number can in some circumstances be linked to a person, and so can be personal data under data protection law.

The project’s approach is that if a way to re-identify individuals from the data is found, the project will:

  1. tell the data owners;
  2. store the data at a level of security suitable for the sensitivity discovered;
  3. make the data and code available only at a suitable level of safeguarding, following the tiered access used by the UK Data Service.

Users must not attempt to identify individuals or their vehicles’ keepers from the data.

10.6 Versions

The data will be revised as the method improves and new MOT data is added. Each build of the pipeline records its versions in session_info.csv, and this book states the build it describes in its preface.

ImportantPitfall: vehicle_id changes between versions

vehicle_id is the row number in the input file and changes whenever a new delivery is processed. To match vehicles across versions, or to other data, use registration_DVSA.
