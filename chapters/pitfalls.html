14  Concepts and pitfalls

This chapter collects, in one place, the things most likely to lead an analysis astray. Each is explained in more detail elsewhere in the book.

14.1 1. Observed is not the same as the fleet

A vehicle is observed in a year when it has a postcode area in that year, which in practice means it had an MOT around then. The observed vehicles are not the whole fleet: vehicles under three, SORN vehicles, most heavy vehicles and most vehicles over 40 are missing (Chapter 1). For cars aged three to about sixteen, the observed count is close to DfT’s count of registered vehicles (Chapter 13).

What to do: say “vehicles observed” or “vehicles tested”, not “vehicles registered” or “the fleet”, and compare with DfT’s figures for ages three and over.

14.2 2. The last three years are incomplete

The number of vehicles observed peaks in 2019 and then falls (Figure 6.1). This is not a shrinking fleet. A new car is not observed until its first MOT at three years old, so cars first registered in 2023 to 2025 are almost entirely missing, and the final year, 2025, is also only partly covered. In 2025 the CARS count of cars of all ages is 0.86 of DfT’s, against 1.1 in 2018.

What to do: for trends in the whole fleet, stop three years before the end of the data, or restrict to vehicles aged three and over. Never read a trend into the final year. Be especially careful with anything that is growing fast among new vehicles, such as electric cars, whose share is understated in recent years.

14.3 3. Location is where the vehicle was tested

The postcode area is that of the MOT testing station (Chapter 7).

What to do: describe results as “vehicles tested in” an area. Do not treat a change of area as a change of address without saying so.

14.4 4. Dead is not scrapped, and unknown is not missing at random

dead means “not tested since”, which includes vehicles scrapped, exported, on SORN or exempt. unknown covers new vehicles before their first test and gaps in a vehicle’s history (Chapter 6).

What to do: call a vehicle that has become dead one that “stopped being tested”, not “scrapped”, unless you have other evidence. Remember that vehicles last seen in the final two years have not yet had the chance to become dead.

14.5 5. Averages depend on who is in the denominator

Average mileage is calculated over the vehicles with a (plausible) mileage that year, a group that changes from year to year and differs from the vehicles counted. Averages of averages are wrong (Chapter 12).

What to do: compute averages as total ÷ count over exactly the group you mean, and report the count alongside.

14.6 6. Mileage needs a test at each end

Annual mileage is shared out from the distance between two tests (Chapter 8). A year after a vehicle’s last test has no mileage; a long gap between tests spreads its distance across the gap, hiding any variation within it. 2020 is particularly affected by delayed tests.

What to do: compare years with similar coverage; be careful with year-on-year changes around 2020, and with vehicles near the start or end of their lives.

14.7 7. Recorded, filled and assumed values

Many harmonised values were filled from similar vehicles or assumed from rules (Chapter 9). Most Euro statuses are assumed from dates; CO2, weight and type approval are often filled from similar vehicles.

What to do: check the _src column, and repeat key results using recorded values only. If the result changes, report both.

14.8 8. Plug-in hybrids are a lower bound

Plug-in hybrids are identified from DfT’s records by model, and models sold both ways are left as ordinary hybrids (Chapter 4).

What to do: report plug-in hybrid numbers as “at least”, or combine all hybrids.

14.9 9. Classifications differ between sources

MOT class 4 is not the same as DfT’s cars (Chapter 3).

What to do: use body_type when comparing with DfT, and state which classification you used.

14.10 10. Postcode areas are big

Postcode areas average over very different places, and patterns between areas may not hold within them (Chapter 7).

What to do: use postcode area results for broad comparisons, not to describe neighbourhoods or individuals.

14.11 11. Northern Ireland is different

Northern Ireland tests at four years in government test centres, its test class is not supplied, and its testing was disrupted in 2020. It is coded NI, a single area.

What to do: consider analysing Great Britain on its own, as the fleet profile tables do.

14.12 12. Identifiers change between versions

vehicle_id is not stable between versions of the data (Chapter 10).

What to do: join on registration_DVSA, and record which version of the data you used.
