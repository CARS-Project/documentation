12  The aggregate tables

The aggregate tables are CSV files that open in any spreadsheet. They answer most common questions without the vehicle table, and they have been built so that results can be combined correctly. This chapter explains the three families of table and the ideas common to all of them. Appendix C lists every file, and each family has a detailed report among the appendices.

12.1 Ideas common to all the tables

Grain

Each table has a grain: the combination of columns that identifies one row. mileage_by_pca_year_body_fuel.csv has one row per year, postcode area, body type and fuel group. Knowing the grain tells you what you can add up: you can sum its rows over fuel groups to get totals by body type, or over postcode areas to get national totals.

Long and wide

Most tables come in long form, with one row per combination and one column per measure, which is best for filtering and pivot tables. Some are also given in wide form, with years (or categories) as columns, which is easier to read and chart. They hold the same numbers.

Counts and totals, not just averages

Where a table gives an average, it also gives the count and total it was calculated from (for example n_legit_mileage and total_legit_mileage).

ImportantPitfall: never average the averages

To get the average mileage of a group of rows, add up their totals and divide by the sum of their counts. Taking the average of the rows’ averages gives each row equal weight, so a postcode area with 2,000 cars counts as much as one with 2 million. Chapter 16 shows the right way in Excel.

Status rows

Postcode area columns include the statuses unborn, unknown and dead as well as real areas, so a table’s rows add up to every vehicle in the vehicle table. For most analyses, filter out the status rows to keep the vehicles observed in each year. Be aware that the share of vehicles that are unknown grows towards the end of the series (Chapter 6).

Plausible mileage

Mileage is reported both in full and for plausible mileage only (Chapter 8). Averages should normally use plausible mileage, as the CARS reports do.

12.2 Mileage tables

Folder tables/mileage/ · report: Vehicles and mileage by postcode area

Vehicles and their annual mileage, for every MOT test class and body type. The most useful single table is mileage_by_pca_year_body_fuel.csv, with 53,123 rows: for each year, postcode area (or status), DfT body type and fuel group, it gives:

Column Meaning
n_vehicles Vehicles
n_with_mileage, n_legit_mileage Vehicles with a recorded, and with a plausible, mileage
n_mileage_removed Vehicles whose mileage was judged implausible and removed
total_mileage, total_legit_mileage Total miles, all recorded and plausible only
n_negative_mileage, n_over_50k_mileage Vehicles with unusual mileages
n_with_age, total_age For calculating average age

The other mileage tables give the same information by age, by MOT test class and by the fuel as DVSA supplied it, and the distribution of mileage in bands.

12.3 Fleet profile tables

Folder tables/profile/ · report: Fleet profile tables

The make-up of the fleet in each year, for cars, light goods vehicles, motorcycles and buses, by characteristics such as fuel, engine size, CO2 band, Euro standard, age band and (for cars) SMMT body type, with the vehicle count and mean mileage of each. They come in long form (profile_categorical_by_year.csv) and in four wide analyses per body type:

  • Analysis 1 (a1_counts_*): vehicles, years by characteristic;
  • Analysis 2 (a2_mean_mileage_*): mean mileage, years by characteristic;
  • Analyses 3 and 4: the same, split by age band.

These tables cover Great Britain only (Northern Ireland is excluded) and count a vehicle in a year only if it has a plausible mileage that year.

12.4 Churn tables

Folder tables/churn/ · report: Churn tables

The churn tables describe how the vehicles in each postcode area change: which vehicles stay, which arrive from elsewhere or new, and which leave by moving or by ceasing to be tested. They cover MOT test class 4 vehicles: cars, vans up to 3,000kg and small minibuses. They are organised in numbered sets:

ImportantPitfall: a ‘move’ is a change of testing area

A vehicle counts as having moved when it is tested in a different postcode area from the year before. That includes vehicles that changed garage without changing address. Moves are also counted only between two years in which the vehicle was observed, so a vehicle that disappears for a year and reappears elsewhere has no move recorded.
