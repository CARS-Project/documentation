Summary

  • Input rows: 138,238,617, covering 2005–2025.
  • Vehicles in scope (existed 2005 onwards): 93,352,241 (67.5% of input rows).
  • Rows sharing a registration with another row: 0.
  • VES records: 76.1% of input rows have one. Of in-scope vehicles with a VES record, 123,155 (0.17%) look like a different vehicle; they are listed in suspected_dvla_mismatches.csv.
  • PCA sequence checks: 0 vehicles recorded dead and later seen in a PCA, and 0 seen and later unborn. Both should be near zero.
  • Make and model: 94.9% of in-scope vehicles have a DfT generic model; 0.8% have a raw model that matched nothing, and 4.3% a placeholder such as UNKNOWN.
  • Against DfT counts (cars): in 2025, 33,273,833 cars were observed here against 38,490,385 licensed or SORN in the DfT statistics (86.4%); for cars first used three or more years earlier, 103.6%. Buses, HGVs and other vehicles are tested outside the MOT scheme, so few are here.
  • Fuel checks: 3,733 fuels corrected to Electric, and 55 fuels implausible for their make/model.
  • Plug-in hybrids: 94.5% of in-scope vehicles have a fuel_DFT from the DfT statistics, and 1,089,905 are plug-in hybrids or range-extended electrics, which neither DVSA nor DVLA distinguishes. 244,402 vehicles are of a make/model that DfT sells both as a plug-in and not, with nothing to say which this one is, so they keep their recorded fuel.
  • Euro status: 91.2% of in-scope vehicles have one, of which 51,515,075 come from the date the standard became compulsory. 3,285,486 vehicles carry a status naming a standard that did not exist when they were made; these are flagged in euro_check, not changed.
  • Mileage: 25,803,313 vehicles have a 2025 mileage, 70.2% of the 36,734,471 in 2024, so 2025 looks like a partial year.
  • Cross-checks: 1,226 of 1,238 checks pass, 1 fail and 11 warn (cross_checks.csv, section below). Every pair of published numbers that must agree is compared; the warnings are plausibility checks against DfT.
  • A location is not always an observation. Bristol gives a vehicle a postcode area from its first registration (copied back from its first MOT) to two years after its last MOT, so the vehicles “in a postcode area” in a year include vehicles not tested that year: see Observation behind the postcode areas below. This is why more cars aged 3+ are placed in a postcode area than DfT counts as licensed or SORN.

Type approval against test class

In-scope vehicles by the family of their harmonised type approval category (type_approval: the DVLA value where the VES record is the same vehicle, else backfilled from similar vehicles) and their harmonised MOT test class (test_class). The two come from different registers, so they check each other: a car (M1) should be class 4 (or 7, a heavy motor caravan), a van (N1) class 4 or 7 depending on its weight, a motorcycle (L1-L3) class 1 or 2 (L2 three-wheel mopeds also 3) and a bus (M2-M3) class 5, or 4 with 9-12 seats. Lorries (N2-N3) are tested outside the MOT scheme, so few appear. The backfill fills the two together and never makes a pair that is not allowed (methodology.md, Section 5.6), so what remains here is in the records; Consistency across variables below has the exact rules.

Consistency across variables

Combinations of two or more variables that cannot, or should not, occur together, checked on every in-scope vehicle (R/consistency.R; methodology.md, Section 9.2). A violation is recorded when every variable involved is the vehicle’s own recorded value, so the conflict is in the source data; otherwise at least one value was backfilled, derived or imputed by the pipeline or by Bristol. Recorded values are never changed, so recorded violations stay; they are listed so that analyses can exclude them.

Enforced rules are ones the backfill guarantees: it never fills a value that breaks them, and the cross-checks fail if it does. The others describe the data; several of them (revenue weight against test class, CO2 against fuel) show where a register is not reliable on its own.

  • n_recorded: violations where every variable is recorded
