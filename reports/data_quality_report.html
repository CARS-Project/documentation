Summary

  • Input rows: 138,238,617, covering 2005–2025.
  • Vehicles in scope (existed 2005 onwards): 93,352,241 (67.5% of input rows).
  • Rows sharing a registration with another row: 0.
  • VES records: 76.1% of input rows have one. Of in-scope vehicles with a VES record, 123,155 (0.17%) look like a different vehicle; they are listed in suspected_dvla_mismatches.csv.
  • PCA sequence checks: 0 vehicles recorded dead and later seen in a PCA, and 0 seen and later unborn. Both should be near zero.
  • Make and model: 94.9% of in-scope vehicles have a DfT generic model; 0.8% have a raw model that matched nothing, and 4.3% a placeholder such as UNKNOWN.
  • Against DfT counts (cars): in 2025, 33,298,028 cars were observed here against 38,490,385 licensed or SORN in the DfT statistics (86.5%); for cars first used three or more years earlier, 103.7%. Buses, HGVs and other vehicles are tested outside the MOT scheme, so few are here.
  • Fuel checks: 3,733 fuels corrected to Electric, and 55 fuels implausible for their make/model.
  • Plug-in hybrids: 94.5% of in-scope vehicles have a fuel_DFT from the DfT statistics, and 1,090,074 are plug-in hybrids or range-extended electrics, which neither DVSA nor DVLA distinguishes. 244,402 vehicles are of a make/model that DfT sells both as a plug-in and not, with nothing to say which this one is, so they keep their recorded fuel.
  • Euro status: 91.3% of in-scope vehicles have one, of which 33,468,440 come from the date the standard became compulsory. 10,332,946 vehicles carry a status naming a standard that did not exist when they were made; these are flagged in euro_check, not changed.
  • Mileage: 25,803,313 vehicles have a 2025 mileage, 70.2% of the 36,734,471 in 2024, so 2025 looks like a partial year.

Vehicles against DfT counts by year and body type

A check of coverage and naming against DfT table VEH0124 (methodology.md, Section 4.8). A ratio near 1 means the MOT data holds about as many vehicles as DfT. Ratios for all ages are below 1 because vehicles under three (four in Northern Ireland) have no MOT; ratio_age3plus is the fairer test. Buses, HGVs and other vehicles are tested outside the MOT scheme, so their ratios are low by design, and motorcycles are low because many are SORN. The make and model breakdown is in dft_stock_by_make_model.csv, and the breakdown by age in dft_stock_by_age.csv.

  • n_mot: in-scope vehicles observed in a postcode area that year (not unborn, dead or unknown), UK
  • n_dft: licensed plus SORN vehicles at the end of the year in VEH0124, UK
  • n_dft_licensed: the licensed part of n_dft
  • ratio_mot_to_dft: n_mot / n_dft
  • n_mot_age3plus: n_mot for vehicles first used at least three years before the year
  • n_dft_age3plus: n_dft for the same ages
  • ratio_age3plus: n_mot_age3plus / n_dft_age3plus
