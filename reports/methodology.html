Methodology

This document describes how MOT-pipline turns the Bristol vehicle-level MOT table into the enhanced vehicle dataset and summary tables. For each stage it sets out what is done, how, and the assumptions behind it, so users of the outputs can judge how far to rely on each column.

The code for each stage is named in its section. Column-level definitions are in outputs/data_dictionary.csv, which the pipeline writes.

All processing of the full table happens in DuckDB (SQL over parquet files). Only small tables come back into R: distinct make/model pairs, make aliases and aggregated results. The pipeline is managed by R targets, which rebuilds only the stages affected by a change.

Principles applied throughout:

  • Supplied values are never overwritten. Cleaned, harmonised or backfilled values go into new columns next to the originals.
  • Every inferred value records its source. Examples are first_use_source, make_method, model_method, *_src, the scope_* flags, dvla_match, fuel_check and engine_check.
  • Weak evidence is flagged, not acted on. A value is only changed when the vehicle’s own records disagree and a second source settles it.
  • Results are deterministic. Ties are broken by explicit rules, and random sampling (sample project only) uses a fixed seed.

2. Input data

Source: annualised_with_ves_4.parquet, produced by the Bristol team and accessed through the RDSF.

Property Value
Rows 138,238,617
Distinct DVSA registrations 138,238,617
Columns 202
Years covered 2005–2025 (2025 is a partial year)

The January 2026 delivery (jan26_annualised_with_2025_anon_and_ves.parquet) differed from the earlier annualised_with_ves.parquet in three ways. It added 2025. It had 5,997 fewer rows, exactly the number of surplus duplicate registrations in the earlier file, so the duplicates were removed. And it renamed every VES column to carry a _DVLA suffix, while replacing the lookup bookkeeping columns (n_api_vehicles_with_vrm, vrm_in_ves, n_ves_obs) with a fuller set that counts the lookups and records what changed between them. The pipeline reads either layout: see resolve_core_columns() in R/stage_core.R.

The file has one row per vehicle, with:

  • Vehicle attributes from the DVSA MOT history API: make, model, fuel, engine size, colours, first used / registration / manufacture dates, and the anonymised test class (test_class_ANON).
  • Per-year columns for each year 2005–2025:
    • mileage_YYYY: annual mileage
    • pca_YYYY: postcode area
    • legit_mileage_YYYY: whether the mileage is plausible
    • is_new_vehicle_YYYY, is_intertest_YYYY, is_end_use_YYYY: status flags
  • DVLA Vehicle Enquiry Service (VES) columns: make, fuel, engine capacity, weight, CO2, Euro status, type approval, wheel plan, tax and MOT status, and key dates. These are attached by registration.
  • VES lookup bookkeeping: how often VES was queried for the registration (n_VES_checks, renamed n_VES_checks_to_linked_DVSA_regs in the October 2026 ves_4 delivery, which also counts the checks against the DVLA registration alone in n_VES_checks_for_DVLA_reg; both include errored checks), how often it answered (n_VES_returns), the first and last of each, whether the last lookup errored (is_error, error), and flags for what changed between lookups: plate_ever_changed_VES, ever_SORN, v5C_issue_date_ever_changed, change_in_other_VES_field and ever_export_marked (a count, not a flag).

Assumptions about the input:

  • Bristol’s annualisation is taken as given. The pipeline does not re-derive annual mileage, postcode area or the per-year flags; see Bristol’s documentation for how they are built. In particular, the mileage columns are treated as annual miles (mean 7,065 in 2018) and are not rescaled.
  • The VES record is not always the same vehicle. Because VES is attached by registration, a registration that has moved to another vehicle brings that vehicle’s record. Section 5.1 detects these.
  • Gaps are common.
    • test_class_ANON is missing for 64.2M rows.
    • firstUsedDate_DVSA is missing for 61.4M rows.
    • VES columns are missing wherever no VES record was found (23.9% of rows).

3. Stage 1: Core vehicle table

Code: R/stage_core.R · Output: outputs/intermediate/01_core.parquet

3.1 Identifiers and duplicates

  • vehicle_id is the row number in the input file. It is stable only for that input file; a new Bristol delivery will renumber vehicles.
  • dup_vrm is TRUE when the DVSA registration appears on more than one row. No registration appears more than once in this input (earlier deliveries had duplicates). Duplicates are kept and flagged, not removed, because it is not known which row is correct.

3.2 Dates

  • birth_date is the earliest of DVSA first used date, registration date and manufacture date. Only 10,030 input vehicles have none of these.
  • first_use_date is the first available of:
    1. DVSA first used date
    2. DVLA month of first registration
    3. birth_date
    first_use_source records which one was used.
  • last_mot_year is the latest year with is_end_use_YYYY = TRUE. Where no year is flagged, it is the last year with a recorded postcode area, unless that year is one of the final two years of data.

Why the fallback: the January 2026 Bristol delivery leaves the end-of-use flag off for most vehicles that stopped being tested. For example, 1.6 million of the 2.4 million vehicles whose last MOT was in 2019 have no flag, against 20 thousand in the earlier delivery. Without the fallback, those vehicles would be unknown for ever, not dead. Where a vehicle has both a flag and a recorded postcode area, the flag year is its last postcode area year for 99.9% of vehicles. A vehicle last seen in the final two years may simply not be due a test yet, so it keeps no last MOT year and stays unknown.

Assumptions:

  • Birth date is the earliest recorded date. For vehicles that sat in stock, or were imported, the manufacture date can be well before first use.
  • The DVLA month of first registration is the first of that month, so it is accurate to the month only.
  • Vehicles with no dates get no invented one. The previous code assigned 2000-01-01 to such vehicles; that placeholder has been removed.

3.3 Postcode area status infill

Bristol’s pca_YYYY is blank in any year where the vehicle was not observed. Each blank is replaced with a status:

Condition (checked in order) pca_YYYY becomes
A postcode area is recorded the postcode area, unchanged
Birth year is the same as or later than year YYYY unborn
The last MOT year (last_mot_year) is earlier than YYYY dead
Otherwise unknown

Assumptions:

  • “Dead” means no longer observed, not scrapped. A vehicle is dead in every year after the year of its last MOT. This includes vehicles that were exported, declared off-road (SORN), or simply stopped being tested.
  • Unobserved years before the last MOT are “unknown”. For example, a new car in its first three years before its first MOT is unknown rather than dead. This prevents vehicles from apparently coming back to life when their first MOT appears.
  • Birth is compared by calendar year. A vehicle born during 2010 with no recorded PCA in 2010 is unborn for 2010.
  • A vehicle with no dates is treated as already born in every year. It is then dead or unknown by the rules above.
  • Recorded postcode areas are used as supplied, including single-letter areas (B, G, L, …). The data covers the whole UK: Northern Ireland is coded NI rather than its postcode area BT (which does not occur), and a small number of vehicles have the code XX, left as supplied.

3.4 Scope: which vehicles are kept

The output aims to include every vehicle that existed at some point from 2005 onwards, including vehicles that are too new to have been tested and vehicles that were never tested. A vehicle is kept if any of these is true (scope start date 2005-01-01, set in config.yml):

Flag Rule Input vehicles meeting it
scope_mileage Total mileage across all years > 0 77,265,716
scope_pca A postcode area recorded in any year 75,437,173
scope_born birth_date on or after the start date 59,048,329
scope_dvla_activity DVLA tax due, last V5C issue, MOT expiry or first registration date on or after the start date 71,810,699

Rule Vehicles kept
Previous code: mileage OR born since 2005 89,014,223

Assumptions:

  • A DVLA record dated 2005 or later is evidence the vehicle existed then. Examples are a V5C issued or tax due in that period.
  • Vehicles with none of these signals are assumed to have left the fleet before 2005. This includes every vehicle with no dates at all.
  • Total mileage is a plain sum across years, so a small negative value can offset a positive one. This is rare.

3.5 Tidying

  • VES columns get a _DVLA suffix where the delivery does not already use one (in earlier files make becomes make_DVLA; the January 2026 file supplies make_DVLA already). The VES lookup bookkeeping columns are carried through under their own names, whichever set the delivery has.
  • engineSize_DVSA is converted to an integer; non-numeric values become missing.
  • The last MOT test date is taken from final_test_date_DVSA and published under that name. DVSA now put a date in the future in lastMotTestDate_DVSA for the untested vehicles DVLA supplies (5.22M of them in the October 2026 file, the latest 2028-12-30); final_test_date_DVSA equals it on every tested vehicle and is missing rather than invented on the rest. A delivery that has only lastMotTestDate_DVSA falls back to it, converted from text to a date.
  • The is_new_vehicle, is_intertest and is_end_use flags are dropped after use. legit_mileage_YYYY is kept for the mileage summaries.

4. Stage 2: Make and model

Code: R/make_model.R · Outputs: outputs/intermediate/dft_*.parquet, outputs/intermediate/make_model_*.parquet, outputs/quality/make_model_review.csv

Makes and models follow the naming of the DfT vehicle licensing statistics: tables VEH0220 (vehicles by make, generic model, model, fuel and engine size) and VEH0124 (by make, generic model, model and year of first use), which DfT builds from the DVLA register. Using the same names means MOT results can be joined to, and checked against, the official counts. No language model is used: every step is a rule, and every result records how it was reached.

Column Meaning

model_final DfT generic model without the make (FIESTA, C CLASS, 1 SERIES)
dft_genmodel The DfT GenModel (FORD FIESTA), for joins to DfT tables

Of 93,352,241 in-scope vehicles, 94.9% have a DfT generic model, 4.3% have a placeholder model such as UNKNOWN (so no model), and 0.8% keep their normalised raw model because nothing matched.

4.1 The DfT reference

inputs/df_VEH0220.csv and inputs/df_VEH0124_AM.csv / _NZ.csv are read as Latin-1 text. Each count is the number of vehicles (licensed plus SORN) at the end of a year, from 2014. To name models, each row is weighted by its largest count in any year, so a model that has since left the fleet still counts.

DfT’s coding has some quirks, which are tidied before matching:

  • Makes spelt two ways are merged. Makes differing only in punctuation or spaces (IVECO-FORD / IVECO FORD) join the larger. inputs/make_aliases.csv merges others: DfT files most Mercedes-Benz vehicles under MERCEDES, and uses MERCEDES-BENZ for recent registrations whose generic model is just the model text.
  • Generic models that are just a model string are recoded. Some recent registrations have a GenModel of the make plus the model string (HYUNDAI TUCSON ADVANCE PHEV AUTO). These model strings are matched like raw text against the rest of the make (TUCSON).
  • A long generic model name that extends a much more common one joins it, when it adds two or more words and has a tenth of the vehicles or fewer.
  • MODEL MISSING is no model.

Four reference tables come out of this (outputs/intermediate/dft_*.parquet): model names and body types, fuel group by engine band (used to settle ambiguous models, Section 4.4), year of first use, and the fuel as DfT records it, which is the only source that separates plug-in hybrids from other hybrids and is used in Section 5.5.

Text that starts with the make’s name (MINI COOPER) is also tried without it. Matches from the prefix, words and fuzzy steps must fit the vehicles’ MOT test class: motorcycles (classes 1 and 2) need a generic model that DfT mostly records as motorcycles, and other classes one it mostly does not. This stops HONDA S-WING 125 becoming the S800 car.

model_confidence is the share of the DfT vehicles under the matched text that have the chosen generic model: 1 for exact and generic, at least 0.9 for prefix and words.

4.4 Choices made per vehicle

For ambiguous and make_only text, each candidate generic model starts from its share of the DfT vehicles under the text. That share is multiplied by how common the vehicle’s fuel group and engine size band, and its year of first use, are among DfT vehicles of that generic model (with add-half smoothing, so a combination DfT never records is unlikely but possible). The candidate with the largest weight is chosen, and model_confidence is its share of the total weight. 936,073 vehicles are chosen this way, and 129,941 vehicles have a model_confidence below 0.5.

Engine size bands follow DfT: up to 50cc, 51–125cc, 126–200cc, then 100cc steps. Fuel groups are petrol, diesel, electric, hybrid (including plug-in and range extended), gas and other.

4.5 Derivative

derivative_final is the raw model text after the words that gave the make and model, kept as recorded apart from case and spacing. FIESTA ZETEC CLIMATE gives ZETEC CLIMATE; 118I M SPORT keeps all its words, because the badge is not part of the generic model name 1 SERIES. It is not standardised: it keeps the detail the DfT scheme drops (Discovery SPORT, C4 GRAND PICASSO) for analyses that need it. A derivative that only repeats the start of the model name (E for E CLASS) is dropped.

4.7 Reviewed decisions and the review list

  • inputs/make_model_overrides.csv is applied last and wins over every step. Each row fixes one raw make/model spelling, gives the DfT make, generic model and derivative, and states the evidence. It is checked when read: a make is required, each raw pair may appear once, every row needs a reason, and a model must be a DfT generic model of the make. It has 15 rows. The first came from the fuel checks in Section 5.3, which caught an earlier lookup giving electric models to old petrol vehicles (PORSCHE TARGA as a Taycan).
  • inputs/make_aliases.csv merges make spellings (Sections 4.1 and 4.2), with a reason for each.
  • outputs/quality/make_model_review.csv lists the raw pairs most worth reviewing, by vehicles: those matched by spelling fixes, word sets or vehicle attributes, or not matched at all, with the three nearest generic models of the make. Adding reviewed rows to the overrides file is how the matching improves.

Edit these files in R or a text editor. A spreadsheet re-saves numbers in its own format (45.160 becomes 45.16, 130E18 becomes 1.3E+020), which changes the raw keys.

5. Stage 3: Record matching, fuel and engine checks, and backfill

Code: R/dvla_match.R, R/fuel_engine.R, R/euro_status.R, R/backfill.R (reference tables) and R/assemble.R (application) · Outputs: outputs/quality/dvla_make_aliases.csv, outputs/quality/suspected_dvla_mismatches.csv, outputs/intermediate/fuel_*.parquet, outputs/intermediate/backfill_*.parquet, then columns in the enhanced table

Eight attributes are harmonised: fuel, engine_cc, revenue_weight, co2, euro_status, type_approval, wheelplan and test_class. The stage runs in four steps:

  1. Check whether each VES record describes the same vehicle (5.1).
  2. Decide the fuel from the vehicle’s own records, then check it against its make/model (5.2, 5.3).
  3. Set the engine size, with 0 for vehicles that have no engine (5.4).
  4. Fill remaining gaps from vehicles with the same fuel (5.5, 5.6).

Figures below are for the 93,352,241 in-scope vehicles.

5.1 DVSA–DVLA record match

The DVSA and VES records of each vehicle are compared in three independent ways:

Comparison Disagrees when
Make The makes differ after removing punctuation, neither is a prefix of the other, and they do not share their first five characters. Known naming pairs (below) agree.
Year Manufacture years differ by more than one year. Where either is missing, first registration years are compared instead.
Engine Both engine sizes are recorded and differ by more than 100cc and by more than 10%.

A comparison that cannot be made counts as agreement. When at least two comparisons disagree, dvla_match is different: the VES record belongs to another vehicle, and none of its values (fuel, engine size, weight, CO2, Euro status, type approval, wheel plan) is used. dvla_evidence counts the disagreements.

Some make pairs are the same vehicle under another name: CHRYSLER-JEEP / JEEP, ROVER / AUSTIN, and motorhome converters such as FIAT / AUTO-TRAIL. They are found from the whole input file. A pair is an alias when it has at least 20 rows with comparable years (checks.dvla_alias_min_rows), and the years agree for at least 90% of them (checks.dvla_alias_min_year_agreement). This finds 392 pairs. The split is clear-cut:

  • Naming pairs agree on year 90–100% of the time.
  • Different makes, in pairs large enough to have qualified, agree 16.4% of the time. For example, BMW / AUDI agree 15.9% of the time.

5.5 Plug-in hybrids: the DfT fuel detail

Neither source distinguishes a plug-in hybrid from any other hybrid. DVSA records Hybrid Electric (Clean) for a mild hybrid, a full hybrid and a plug-in alike, and Electric Diesel for both kinds of diesel hybrid; the VES fuel types map onto the same two labels. The DfT vehicle licensing statistics do distinguish them: table VEH0220, already read for the make/model naming scheme (Section 4.1), records eleven fuels, among them PLUG-IN HYBRID ELECTRIC (PETROL), PLUG-IN HYBRID ELECTRIC (DIESEL) and RANGE EXTENDED ELECTRIC.

VEH0220 counts vehicles rather than naming them, so the detail is attached by make and model (R/fuel_dft.R). Every DfT fuel and every harmonised fuel is put into the same set of narrow groups, and a vehicle is only ever matched against DfT rows in its own group:

5.7 Euro status from the registration date

A Euro status is recorded for only a minority of vehicles. The VES returns one mostly for vehicles first registered from 2018 onwards, so after the variant backfill of Section 5.6 44.6% of in-scope vehicles still had none.

The standards were phased in on fixed dates, though, and a vehicle cannot have been registered new in Great Britain after the date a standard became compulsory without meeting it. So where nothing else says otherwise, a missing Euro status takes the standard in force when the vehicle was made (R/euro_status.R, euro_status_src = euro_schedule).

Three decisions make that assumption as weak as the rules allow:

  • The compulsory dates, not the type-approval dates. Each standard opened for new type approvals a year or more before it became compulsory for all new registrations. Using the later date means a vehicle may well meet a newer standard than it is given here, but it cannot meet an older one.
  • The date the vehicle was made, not its British registration date. birth_date is the earliest of the DVSA first-used, registration and manufacture dates (Section 3.2), which for an import is the manufacture date rather than the later British first registration. Tested against the 11.3 million vehicles whose birth_date is more than a year before their first_use_date, birth_date errs towards an older standard and first_use_date towards a newer one, so birth_date is used (first_use_date only where birth_date is missing).

Vans were a year or two behind cars, lorries and buses ran on the heavy-duty timetable, and motorcycles reached Euro 4 only in 2017. Applying the car dates to motorcycles would be too new for 99.98% of the motorcycles that do have a recorded status.

5.9 Assumptions and limitations

  1. Two disagreements between DVSA and VES mean different vehicles. One disagreement is common for genuine matches: imports registered years after manufacture, and engine sizes recorded differently. A registration moved between two vehicles of the same make and year, where one engine size is missing, is not detected; the fuel check catches some of these.
  2. DVSA fuel is trusted unless another record contradicts it. VES overrides it only for “Other”, and for Electric / Fuel Cells vehicles that VES shows as hybrids or as having an engine.
  3. A recorded engine size on an Electric vehicle is evidence of an engine, unless VES confirms it is electric (for example a converted classic that keeps its original engine size on record), or the make/model is electric-only.
  4. Vehicles with no engine have engine size 0. Range-extender and hybrid vehicles recorded as Electric by both DVSA and VES, with no engine size, get
  5. Rare fuels are flagged, not corrected. LPG conversions, electric conversions of classic cars and uncommon factory variants are all legitimate, so only electric-only models, where the records disagree, are corrected.
  6. The fuel check depends on the make/model cleaning. Merged or wrong models produce rare_for_model and implausible_for_model flags; these point to raw spellings worth reviewing. Small make/models (under 100 vehicles) are not checked, and on the 1% sample most make/models are no_reference.
  7. A model’s DfT fuel is its vehicles’ fuel. The plug-in distinction of Section 5.5 is a property of the model string, not of the individual vehicle, so it is only as good as the make/model match and cannot find a plug-in hybrid of a model DfT records both ways. The plug-in counts are a lower bound.
  8. A vehicle registered new after a standard became compulsory meets it. The Euro status of Section 5.7 is an assumption about the rules, not an observation of the vehicle. It is wrong for used imports and rebuilt vehicles, which take a British registration long after they were built; birth_date limits that, and euro_status_src separates assumed values from recorded ones so they can be dropped.
  9. Vehicles of the same make, model, birth year and fuel share attributes. This is reasonable for wheel plan, type approval and test class. It is weaker for CO2 and weight, which vary with trim, gearbox and body style.
  10. Birth year stands in for model year.
  11. At the variant level, engine size must match to the cc. Near-identical engines (1596 cc vs 1598 cc) form separate groups; the model-year-fuel level then applies.
  12. A vehicle with a backfilled engine size matches the most common variant of its make, model, year and fuel, and takes that variant’s attributes.
  13. A single recorded vehicle is enough to fill a group. This follows from min_vehicles = 1.
  14. Missing values are not random. VES values are mostly missing where no VES record was found, which is more common for older and scrapped vehicles. Their gaps are filled from vehicles that do have records.
  15. “Other” is treated as one fuel group, although it covers a mix of fuels.
  16. Euro status spellings are standardised, not interpreted (5.6). The letters after the standard are kept together, because the stage letter and the emission code cannot be told apart once the spacing varies: “EURO 6D AR” and “EURO 6 DAR” are both “EURO 6 DAR”, and stay separate from “EURO 6 AR”. Other misspellings still count as separate values. The derived euro_standard column (Section 6.1) is more robust.

For analysis sensitive to these assumptions, filter to recorded values with *_src IN ('dvsa', 'dvla'), or to fuel_check = 'ok'. The churn tables use the recorded DVSA fuel, engine size and test class columns by default.

  • Real PCA: any pca_YYYY value other than dead, unborn or unknown. NI (Northern Ireland) is a real PCA; churn.exclude_ni_rows removes it from table rows while still counting moves to and from it.

  • Status relative to a reference year:

    Status Meaning
    dead dead in the comparison year
    unborn unborn in the comparison year
    same same postcode area as the reference year
    different anything else, including a different area and unknown

  • Age is whole years at 1 June of the year: floor((1 June − first_use_date) in days / 365.25).

  • Bands (age, mileage, engine size) include their lower bound and exclude their upper bound. Engine 2000 cc is in “1700–2000”; 2001 cc is “>2000”.

7.2 Movement measures

  • A move is a change of postcode area between two consecutive years in which both years hold a real PCA. A sequence such as LS → unknown → BD therefore counts no moves.
  • movement_rate is the number of moves between 2007 and 2017 divided by (age in 2017 − 3). It is only calculated for vehicles aged 4 or more in 2017.
  • movement_rate_window is the same number of moves divided by the number of consecutive-year pairs in which the vehicle was in a real PCA in both years.
  • movement_rate_2022 is the same as movement_rate for 2012–2022.
  • static_2017 means in the same real PCA in 2016, 2017 and 2018.
  • Per-vehicle history:
    • first_real_pca: the first real PCA seen from 2005.
    • birth_pca: the first real PCA immediately after an unborn year. It is missing for vehicles already alive in 2005.
    • ever_moved_pca: ever seen in a real PCA other than the first one.
  • Clean Air Zone category:
    • petrol_pre2006: petrol, first used before 2006, a proxy for pre-Euro 4.
    • diesel_pre2015: diesel, first used before 2015, a proxy for pre-Euro 6.
    • other: everything else.

7.3 Assumptions

  • (age − 3) is the time a vehicle has been observable. This assumes a first MOT at three years old. Northern Ireland tests at four, and early tests happen.

  • movement_rate understates movement for vehicles older than about 14. The numerator covers only the 11-year window, but the denominator keeps growing with age. movement_rate_window is the check for older vehicles.

  • different mixes two things: genuine moves and vehicles that became unobserved (unknown).

  • The CAZ categories use first use date, not the Euro status. Set 7 includes a cross-check of the proxy against the harmonised euro_status, which now covers 91.3% of in-scope vehicles against 14.5% with a VES value. Read that cross-check with care: since Section 5.7 fills a missing Euro status from the date the vehicle was made, most of the Euro statuses it compares are themselves derived from a date, so agreement is partly built in. euro_status_src on the enhanced table separates the recorded values, and churn.euro_column can be set to euroStatus_DVLA to check against recorded values only.

  • The data end in 2025, and 2025 is partial. 25,803,313 vehicles have a 2025 mileage against 36,734,471 in 2024, so a vehicle last seen in 2024 may simply not have been tested yet. Measures described as “left by” a year are measured against the last year in years.

  • The churn tables use the harmonised attributes by default (churn.test_class_column, churn.fuel_column, churn.engine_column, churn.euro_column), so they cover more vehicles than the values as supplied and carry the corrections of Section 5. Each setting takes the churn v2 column instead (test_class_ANON, fuel_DVSA, engineSize_DVSA, euroStatus_DVLA) to reproduce the old tables. What this changes, for class 4 vehicles:

    Attribute As supplied Harmonised
    Test class 86.4% 100%
    Engine size 88.7% 100%
    Euro status 14.1% 59.1%
    Fuel 100% 100% (0.3% of values corrected)

  • The Set 7 Euro status check column is euro_status, named euro_status_recorded in churn v2, because the value may now be backfilled rather than recorded.

  • An engine size of 0 cc is its own band, 0 (electric). The harmonised engine_cc is 0 for the fuels with no engine, so the band is electric vehicles (1.66M class 4) and a handful of fuel cell vehicles. They are not small-engined vehicles, so the lowest size band starts at 1 cc and is named for that: 1-1400 in the 3 and 4 band breakdowns, 1-600 in the 100 cc breakdown and 1-1000 in the detailed one. Churn v2 treated 0 cc as missing. Set 3 gains an n_engine_zero column; n_engine_lt1400 keeps its name and now counts 1-1399 cc.

  • In churn v2 reproduction mode (engine_column: engineSize_DVSA) the same band instead holds every 0 cc recording, which is 48,800 diesel and 294 petrol vehicles as well as 11,550 electric ones.

7.5 Life path charts

Code: R/lifepath_alluvial.R · Outputs: outputs/figures/lifepath_alluvial/lifepath_alluvial_<PCA>.png

One alluvial chart per postcode area, ported from MOT-data-analysis/Rscripts/Churn/lifepath_alluvial.R. Each chart covers 2015-2019 (lifepath.years) and shows every vehicle in the area in at least one of those years, as long as it is not already dead in the first year or still unborn in the last.

  • A ribbon is one trajectory: a distinct sequence of states across the years, not one vehicle, and its width is the number of vehicles following it. Leeds has about 200 of them.
  • The states are the area itself, split into vehicles in it every year (stable) and in it in only some years (transient), any other postcode area, and the unborn, unknown and dead statuses of Section 3.3. Those statuses are what keep every year’s total the same, so nothing enters or leaves the chart.
  • Colour is the trajectory type: always in the area, there at the start and end, arriving (new or from elsewhere), leaving (scrapped or to elsewhere), or passing through. Flows that only carry a vehicle from unborn to unborn, or from dead to dead, are drawn faintly, because they are not movements.
  • Population: test class 4, using the harmonised test_class (lifepath.test_class_column). This setting is separate from the churn one so that the charts can cover Northern Ireland, whose vehicles have no anonymised test class at all.
  • Area names in the titles come from inputs/postcode_area_names.csv. The code XX gets no chart, as it is not a postcode area.

Assumptions:

  • “Scrapped” means the trajectory ends dead, which is the end of MOT testing and not necessarily scrapping (Section 3.3).
  • A vehicle counted as stable is stable only within the chart years. It may have moved before or after them.

8. Stage 6: Mileage summaries

Code: R/summary_mileage.R · Outputs: outputs/tables/mileage/

Method: for each year, every vehicle with a recorded mileage_YYYY contributes one row, and the rows are summed into:

  • total annual mileage, vehicle count and mean by PCA (or status), year and the Bristol legit_mileage flag
  • total mileage by legit flag and year
  • total mileage by test class and year, with class labels
  • total mileage by DVSA fuel and year

Each breakdown is written in long form and wide form (years as columns).

Area summaries for the mileage report (build_mileage_area_summaries(), target mileage_area_tables) hold sums and counts, so any grouping can be rebuilt from them:

  • mileage_by_pca_year_body_fuel.csv: by year, pca_YYYY value (postcode area or status), body type and fuel_simple: vehicles; vehicles with a mileage, with a mileage flagged plausible, and with a mileage Bristol removed; total and plausible mileage; negative and over-50,000 mileages; and the total and count of ages (the year minus the first use year), with negative ages counted apart.
  • mileage_by_age_year_body_fuel.csv: observed vehicles (a real PCA) by year, body type, fuel and age (capped at 40; -1 for a negative age), with their plausible mileage.
  • mileage_distribution_by_year_body.csv: recorded mileage in 1,000-mile bands up to 50,000, then wider bands, by year, body type and flag.

These feed outputs/reports/mileage_report.md (Section 9), which also uses postcode area boundaries (inputs/postcode_areas_100m.gpkg) and DfT small-area vehicle counts added up to postcode areas (inputs/dft_lsoa_vehicles_by_pca.csv); inputs/README.md describes how both were built.

Assumptions:

  • Mileage is annual as supplied. It is not rescaled or capped. Negative and very high values are included; the quality report counts them.
  • Bristol has already removed implausible mileage. Where legit_mileage_YYYY is FALSE, mileage_YYYY is empty. A recorded mileage either has the flag TRUE (plausible) or has no flag (not assessed; up to about 5% of recorded mileage a year, and more in a partial final year). The legit_mileage groups in the tables above are therefore TRUE and missing.
  • Vehicles are grouped by their status in that year. A vehicle recorded as unknown in a year that still has mileage appears under unknown.

10. Reproducibility

  • The whole pipeline is defined in _targets.R. It runs with targets::tar_make(), and only stages whose code, settings or inputs have changed are rebuilt. Editing a report template re-renders only that report.
  • All settings that change results are in config.yml.
  • The reference inputs are version-controlled in inputs/, with their source recorded in inputs/README.md.
  • Package versions are recorded in renv.lock.
  • Results are deterministic:
    • backfill and fuel ties are broken by value
    • make/model matching has no random element, and per-vehicle model choices break ties by candidate order
    • the sample project draws a 1% Bernoulli sample of input rows with seed 42
  • The make aliases are derived from the input, so a new Bristol delivery can change them slightly; they are written out for inspection.
  • vehicle_id depends on row order in the input file. Join to external data on registration_DVSA, not vehicle_id.

11. Validation

The unit tests run on every change. The other checks below were made once, when the pipeline was first built against the earlier annualised_with_ves.parquet delivery; their figures are a record of that validation and are not recomputed.

  • Unit tests (tests/testthat/) use small synthetic vehicles to check:
    • PCA status infill
    • the scope rule
    • date coalescing and duplicate flags
    • reading either Bristol delivery’s column names
    • make/model matching to the DfT names: each matching step, the vehicle-type check, derivatives, per-vehicle choices and the overrides file
    • DVSA–DVLA matching, with and without make aliases
    • the fuel rules: a mismatched VES record, hybrids recorded as Electric or Fuel Cells, fuel from engine size, corrections on an electric-only model, an LPG conversion, a removed engine size and a 0cc engine
    • backfill tie-breaking, minimum support and same-fuel matching
    • the Euro status schedule: the standard in force for each body type and date, that a recorded value is never overwritten, that a value too new for the vehicle is flagged, and that body types with no schedule stay missing
    • the DfT fuel detail: that no fuel group mixes two energy sources, that a model string settles a plug-in where the generic model cannot, that models DfT sells both ways are left alone, and that a plug-in match is refused for a vehicle with no engine size
    • band edges against R’s cut()
    • movement rates and PCA history
    • the Set 3 identity
  • The Euro status schedule and the DfT fuel detail were both profiled on the full input before being added. The schedule was compared with every vehicle that has a recorded Euro status, one body type at a time, and the choice of date, of schedule and of which body types to leave alone all come from that comparison (Section 5.7). The DfT fuel detail was checked for how often a model string and a generic model settle the plug-in question before the 0.9 threshold was set (Section 5.5).
  • DVSA–DVLA matching was profiled on the full input before choosing the rule. Year agreement was 99.95% for vehicles with the same make, 96–100% for naming pairs, and about 20% for genuinely different makes. Section 5.1 gives the current figures.
  • Plate decoding returned identical plate type, date and location to DVLAAPI::registration_analysis() for 40,038 registrations, and identical vanity flags.
  • Make/model cleaning was first a port of the legacy rules plus an AI lookup, audited in make_model_cleaning_audit.md. It has been replaced by the DfT naming in Section 4, whose comparison with DfT counts (Section 4.8) is rebuilt with every run.
  • Enhanced table against the legacy enhanced parquet on a 1% sample: for all 874,629 vehicles present in both, pca_YYYY in every year, plate type, date and location, the vanity flag and birth date were identical.
  • Churn tables on the same 1% sample: the legacy make_churn_output_tables_v2.R and this pipeline produced identical values in all 70 output files.
  • Full-data comparison (tests/compare_legacy.R) against the legacy enhanced parquet and churn v2 tables:
    • Every legacy vehicle is present. Of the 87,476,599 legacy vehicles, 87,464,875 matched a non-duplicated vehicle. The other 11,724 were also present but flagged dup_vrm, so the comparison left them out.
    • Matched vehicles agreed completely. For all 87,464,875, pca_YYYY in every year, plate type, date and location, the vanity flag and birth date were 100% identical.
    • The new table had 3,832,000 more vehicles, all from the wider scope rule. 3,747,116 of them qualified only through DVLA activity since 2005.
    • Churn tables differed only as the wider scope implies. Most cells differed by less than 1%. The largest absolute differences (about 55,000) were in the dead / unborn status counts of Sets 0 and 1, where the added vehicles fall. Large relative differences only occurred in cells that were near zero.
