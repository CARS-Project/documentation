Fleet profile tables

NoteGenerated by the pipeline

This appendix is outputs/reports/fleet_profile_report.md from the MOT-pipline repository, copied unchanged into the book by scripts/sync-pipeline.R. Paths such as outputs/... refer to that repository. See Appendix C — Maintaining this book.

The tables in tables/profile/ answer the four analyses in the mileage analysis request: the fleet by year and body type, broken down by vehicle characteristic, counted (Analyses 1 and 3) and averaged over mileage (Analyses 2 and 4), with and without an age band.

They are built by R/summary_profile.R from vehicles_enhanced.parquet, and cover 2005–2025 for Buses and coaches, Cars, Light goods vehicles and Motorcycles.

What is counted

A vehicle is in a table for a year when all of these hold:

  • its body type is one of Buses and coaches, Cars, Light goods vehicles and Motorcycles (fleet_profile.body_types)
  • it was in a real postcode area that year, that is not unborn, dead or unknown
  • it was not in Northern Ireland (exclude_ni: TRUE)
  • its mileage that year passes mileage_filter (legit). legit keeps only the vehicle-years whose mileage Bristol judged plausible.

The mileage filter is per vehicle-year, not per vehicle. A vehicle counts in the years its mileage was judged plausible and not in the others, so the same vehicle can appear in 2018 and not in 2019.

Every characteristic’s categories add up to the same vehicle count, so the columns of an Analysis 1 table reconcile within each characteristic block. Where a characteristic is not known the vehicles are counted under (missing) rather than dropped.

The long tables carry counts and totals, not means alone, so any coarser grouping can be derived from them without rebuilding: mean_mileage is total_mileage / n_with_mileage.

Engine size is banded on recorded cc, with no rounding

A car recorded as 1,390cc is in <1.4, not in the 1.4 it is sold as. Rounding to the nearest tenth of a litre first would move 7.54M cars, 10.2% of those with an engine size, up a band — 6.69M of them from <1.4 to 1.4-1.7, driven by common capacities such as 1,390cc, 1,360cc, 1,396cc and 1,398cc. Exactly 2,000cc is in 1.7-2.0, as in the churn tables.

The bands differ by body type: litres for cars, vans and buses, and the tax classes for motorcycles.

CO2 of 0 only exists for electric vehicles

A recorded 0 g/km is impossible for anything with an engine, so it is cleared early in the pipeline, before the backfill (R/fuel_engine.R), and the vehicle is filled from similar vehicles instead. The co2 here is therefore either a real value or missing, and only Electric and Fuel Cells vehicles reach the 0 band. co2_check in vehicles_enhanced.parquet says which vehicles were affected.

CO2 is reported for motorcycles and buses, but barely exists

Read the CO2 rows for these body types with care. They are produced because they were asked for, not because the data supports them. Across the whole fleet 93% of recorded motorcycle CO2 values and 86% of recorded bus values are 0, so once the zeros are treated as missing almost nothing is left, and what remains is not a representative sample. Means and bands for these two body types should not be quoted without this caveat.

Motorcycles 2025 644,786 69.5
Buses and coaches 2025 21,803 5.4

Euro standard is mostly derived, not recorded

Where the Euro status of a car came from, 2025

dvla is recorded; variant and model_year_fuel are backfilled from similar vehicles; euro_schedule is the standard that was compulsory for new registrations of that body type when the vehicle was made. Only the dvla rows are measurements, so a cross-tabulation of Euro standard against age largely reproduces the age variable.

SMMT body type covers cars only

SMMT classification coverage, 2025

The RACF reference (uk_fleet_enriched_v2) holds DfT cars only, so the SMMT type is applied to cars and left missing for every other body type (fleet_profile.smmt_body_types). Matching it to vans and minibuses would classify them from whichever car shares their generic model name, which is wrong rather than merely incomplete.

A vehicle takes the SMMT type of its DfT model string where that name is consistent enough (smmt_min_share), and of its generic model otherwise — the same rule as body_type.

Known limits

Young vehicles are missing from the recent years

A vehicle’s mileage is only known once it has had an MOT, at three years old, so the youngest age bands empty out towards the end of the series. In 2019 there were 1.50M vehicles aged 0 with a recorded postcode area; in 2024 there were 12,703, and in 2025, 2,576.

The 0-2 age band is not usable for the last three years, and the vehicles that do appear there are a biased sample: only those tested unusually early, which are the high-mileage ones. Mean mileage at age 1 was 8,599 in 2019 and 18,608 in 2025 for that reason.

2025 is also a partial year.

Means mix recorded and backfilled values

engine_cc, co2 and revenue_weight are recorded where the DVLA supplied them and otherwise backfilled from the most common value of similar vehicles. For cars, 61.0% of CO2 and only 27.0% of weights are recorded. The *_src columns in vehicles_enhanced.parquet give the provenance per vehicle; the profile tables do not split by it, so a mean is over the mix.

Revenue weight is effectively absent for motorcycles — 2.18% coverage, and only 652 of 3.94M recorded by the DVLA — so revenue_weight should not be read for that body type at all.

Buses and coaches are mostly minibuses

There are only 138,716 in the whole dataset, most of them MOT class 5, so cells split by year, age band and engine size are frequently in single figures.
