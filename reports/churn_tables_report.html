Churn tables: what each one contains and exactly how it was made

NoteGenerated by the pipeline

This appendix is outputs/reports/churn_tables_report.md from the MOT-pipline repository, copied unchanged into the book by scripts/sync-pipeline.R. Paths such as outputs/... refer to that repository. See Appendix C — Maintaining this book.

Generated from reports/churn_tables_report.qmd by the MOT-pipline churn_tables_report target (profile: main). Edit the template, not this file: every number below is recomputed from the data each time the pipeline runs.

1. What this document is for

The churn tables answer the question “do vehicles stay in the same part of the country, and does that differ by age, mileage, fuel or engine size?” There are 73 CSV files in outputs/tables/churn/. This document explains, for each one:

  • which vehicles are counted (and which are left out),
  • which variables were used, how they were grouped, and what was calculated,
  • what every column means,
  • how complete the underlying variables are, and how their values are spread,
  • the traps worth knowing about before quoting a number.

No programming knowledge is assumed. Where a rule is fussy, the fussy version is given rather than a simplified one, because the simplified version is where mistakes come from. Column and file names appear like this.

2. The short version

  • Every table is built from one intermediate file, outputs/intermediate/churn_base.parquet, which has one row per vehicle (81,112,156 vehicles) and one column per year for location and mileage.
  • A vehicle’s location in a year is a postcode area (“LS”, “BS”, “BT”), or one of three words standing in for “no location recorded”: unborn, dead, unknown.
  • Almost every table counts vehicles grouped by postcode area, and splits them either by year or by a characteristic (age, mileage, fuel, engine size, emissions category).
  • The biggest limitation is not missing data in the usual sense: it is what a “location” is. The annualisation gives a vehicle a postcode area in every year from its first registration to two years after its last MOT: the years before the first MOT (at age three) copy the first test’s area, and the two years after the last one carry it on. So a vehicle’s area in a year is not always a year in which it was tested there, and a scrapped vehicle stays in its area’s stock for about two years (section 5 and section 12). In 2019, the best covered year, 52.3% of the churn-base vehicles had a postcode area.
  • The pipeline cross-checks every count that two tables should agree on (outputs/quality/cross_checks.csv, summarised in the data quality report): the Set 2 stock, Set 1b, Set 1d, Set 3, Set 4, Set 7A, 7B and 7C totals all reconcile with the churn base.

3. Where the data comes from

3.1 The chain of files

The churn tables are the last step of a longer pipeline. Each step writes a file; the next step reads it. Nothing in the churn step goes back to the original data.

4. The one table everything is built from

churn_base.parquet has one row per vehicle. Three kinds of column:

  1. Identifiers and dates copied or derived from the cleaned file.
  2. Year columns: one pca_YYYY (location) and one mileage_YYYY (miles driven that year) per year, 41 columns in all.
  3. Precomputed measures: age, move counts, movement rates, bands, flags. These are computed once here so that every table uses an identical definition. If two tables disagree, it is not because they measured differently.

5. The vocabulary, pedantically

Postcode area (PCA) - the letters at the start of a UK postcode: “BS” for Bristol, “LS” for Leeds. There are about 120 of them. Northern Ireland is the single area BT (earlier deliveries called it NI).

A real postcode area - any value that is not unborn, dead or unknown. The phrase is used throughout: “vehicles in a real PCA in 2017” means vehicles whose location in 2017 was actually known.

unborn / dead / unknown - filled in where no postcode area was recorded, by these rules, in this order:

  1. unborn if the vehicle’s birth_date year is later than the year in question;
  2. dead if the vehicle’s last MOT year is earlier than the year in question;
  3. unknown otherwise - alive, but not observed.

A vehicle with no dates at all is treated as already born.

Status in a comparison - when a table compares a vehicle’s location in one year against a reference year, the answer is one of four words, tested in this order:

Status Meaning
dead the comparison year says dead
unborn the comparison year says unborn
same a real postcode area, the same as the reference year
different anything else: a genuinely different area, or unknown, or - where the reference year is the earlier one (Sets 1b, 1c) - a reference-year status of unborn, unknown or dead

The last row is the single most important caveat in this document. different means “not demonstrably in the same place”, not “moved”. Two things other than a move put a vehicle in it:

  • A vehicle not located in the comparison year (unknown) is counted as different, not as missing. This is rare: 33,656 vehicles arrived in an area in 2018 from unknown or dead.
  • In Sets 1b and 1c a new vehicle is different. The status describes the later year against the earlier one, so a vehicle that was unborn a year earlier and is now in an area is different - it did not move, it was registered. In 2018, 2.57M of the 8.57M different vehicles in Set 1b (lag 1) were new vehicles; 5.97M had moved from another area. Set 1d separates these flows; use it for any statement about moving.

A move - a change of postcode area between two consecutive years where both years hold a real postcode area. A vehicle going LS → unknown → BD is recorded as no moves, because neither pair of consecutive years has two real areas. This makes the move counts conservative: real moves across a gap in observation are not counted.

Age - whole years at 1 June of the year in question, calculated as the days from the first use date to 1 June divided by 365.25, rounded down. A vehicle first used in August 2013 is age 3 in 2017, not 4. Ages can be negative (a first use date after the year), which is why the age bands include a <0 band.

Movement rate - the churn v2 definition, n_moves_2007_2017 / (age in 2017 - 3), defined for vehicles aged 4 or over in 2017. The idea was “moves per year of life after the first three, when a car has no MOT”. It does not measure that, for two reasons, and it should not be compared across ages:

  • The annualisation back-fills a vehicle’s postcode area into the years before its first MOT, so those years are “located” and a move between them can be counted (though, being copies, they rarely show one). The numerator therefore counts moves over up to ten year pairs (2007-2017) while the denominator counts years of life after the third.
  • For a vehicle aged 4 the denominator is 1 but there are about 4.5 observed year pairs; for a vehicle aged 20 it is 17 but there are at most 10.

In this build the mean churn v2 rate falls from 0.47 at age 4 to 0.05 at age 20 and over, while the windowed rate stays between 0.1 and 0.2 at every age: the fall is produced by the formula, not by the vehicles. The churn v2 rate is larger than the windowed rate below age 13 and smaller above it. Windowed movement rate divides by the number of year pairs where both years hold a real area (n_moves_2007_2017 / n_obs_2007_2017), so it measures moves per observed year and is the one to quote. Note that the observed pairs include the back-filled years before the first MOT and the two years after the last one, in which no move can appear, so even the windowed rate understates the rate at which vehicles that are on the road change area.

Static - a vehicle in a real postcode area in 2017 that was in the same area in 2016 and 2018. A vehicle unknown in 2016 is therefore not static, even if it never moved: non-static mixes movers with vehicles not located in 2016 or 2018 (or dead by 2018). Of the 2017 cohort aged 4+, 64.8% were static.

Bands - every band includes its lower bound and excludes its upper bound. Engine size 2000cc falls in 1700-2000; 2001cc falls in >2000. Mileage 12,000 falls in >12k, not 5-12k. Engine size 0 has a band of its own, 0 (electric), because the harmonised engine size is 0 for the fuels with no engine; the lowest size band starts at 1cc and says so.

The NA column - several wide tables have a final column headed NA. That is not an error: it counts vehicles whose characteristic is unknown (usually engine size or mileage). Tables that list one row per category instead drop the unknown category. So a band table and its matching cross-tab can have slightly different totals, and the difference is the unknowns.

6. How complete the variables are

The table in section 4.1 gives the exact missing counts. In words, for the 81,112,156 vehicles:

  • Location and fuel are never missing. Every vehicle has a value in every pca_YYYY column (though 49.0% of 2017’s values are one of the three stand-in words), and every vehicle has a fuel group.
  • Engine size is missing for 6,232 vehicles (0.0%), so the four engine band columns are missing for exactly the same vehicles.
  • Euro status is missing for 3,888,410 vehicles (4.8%); in the harmonised column these hold the reason instead (pre_euro, no_schedule or no_date), so count them by euro_status_src, not by an empty euro_status. That looks like good coverage, but most of it is an assumption rather than a record: the register holds a Euro status for only a minority of vehicles, and where it holds none the enhanced table fills in the standard that was compulsory for new registrations of that body type when the vehicle was made (methodology.md, Section 5.7). Euro status is used only in Set 7, where it decides the Clean Air Zone category together with the first use date.
  • Movement rate is missing for 30,878,242 vehicles (38.1%) - those aged under 4 in 2017, for whom it is not defined.
  • birth_pca (the area a vehicle first appeared in) is missing for 41,617,674 vehicles (51.3%): vehicles already registered before the data starts never have an unborn-to-area transition to read it from. Set 4’s “born here” columns are only meaningful for recent vehicles.
  • The dates behind the age calculation are never missing in the combined first_use_date, but the individual sources are: DVSA’s own date is missing for 10,093,594 vehicles (12.4%), which is what Set 0 reports on.

Set 1 - who is still where they were

  • Question. Of the vehicles in area X in a reference year, how many are in the same area, a different one, dead or not yet born, in each later year?
  • Vehicles. All of them, including those whose reference-year location is dead, unborn or unknown - except in set1a2, which keeps only vehicles in a real area in 2017 (41,341,397 vehicles).
  • Variables. pca_YYYY for every year, test_class, and age for the age4plus variants.
  • Grouping. Row = reference-year postcode area (plus test class). For each year, every vehicle is labelled same, different, dead or unborn against the reference year, and the labels are counted.
  • Arithmetic. Counts only. The table is then pivoted so each status-and-year pair becomes a column, named status_year (for example same_2019), ordered by year then status, with missing combinations as 0.
  • Files and what the reference year is.
    • set1a_...change_from_2017.csv - reference year 2017, all vehicles. Rows include dead, unborn and unknown as reference locations.
    • set1a2_..._cohort_only.csv - the same, restricted to vehicles in a real area in 2017. This is the one to use for “of the 2017 fleet, how many stayed”.
    • set1b_...from_year-N.csv for N = 1, 2, 3 - a rolling comparison: each year against the area N years earlier. The row label is the later year’s area, so a vehicle contributes to different rows in different years.
    • set1c_...from_year-N_age4plus.csv for N = 1, 2 - as 1b, but only vehicles aged 4 or over in the later year, which removes the cars-with-no-MOT-yet effect.
    • set1d_pca_flows_year-1.csv - the year-on-year flows behind 1b (lag 1), one row per year and area: stock_prev (in the area a year earlier), stayed, out_moved (now in another area), out_dead, out_unobserved, in_moved (from another area), in_new (unborn a year earlier), in_reappeared (unknown or dead a year earlier) and stock_now. Exactly, stock_prev = stayed + out_moved + out_dead + out_unobserved and stock_now = stayed + in_moved + in_new + in_reappeared; nationally, in_moved equals out_moved. Added in the October 2026 audit.
  • Traps. unknown is inside different, and in 1b and 1c so are new vehicles (section 5): 1b’s different is not a count of movers - Set 1d’s in_moved is. The test_class column is constant (4) because of the class filter; it is kept so the file shape matches the earlier analysis.

11. How to rebuild this

The churn tables and this document are built by the pipeline, so they always describe the same run:

# everything
targets::tar_make()

# just the churn tables and this document
targets::tar_make(names = tidyselect::starts_with("churn"))

Changing a setting under churn: in config.yml rebuilds the tables that depend on it and then this document. Editing the band definitions or the documentation text means editing R/summary_churn.R, R/report_churn.R or reports/churn_tables_report.qmd - never the generated .md file, which is overwritten on every run.

12. Things to be careful about, in one place

  1. different includes unknown. In Set 1 and among Set 7B’s leavers, a vehicle that was simply not seen counts as not being in the same place (Set 7B shows these separately as unknown).
  2. Moves are only counted between two observed years. A move across a gap in observation is invisible, so move counts and movement rates are floors, not estimates.
  3. A location is not always an observation. A car under three has no MOT, and the annualisation copies its first test’s postcode area back to its first registration; after the last MOT, the area is carried on for two years before the vehicle becomes dead. So a young vehicle cannot be seen to move before its first test, and a scrapped vehicle stays in its area’s stock for about two years. Against DfT’s licensed + SORN counts, the cars aged 3+ located in a postcode area are 6-19% too many, depending on the year (data quality report, cross-checks). The last two or three years of data are also incomplete, because recent first tests are not yet back-filled.
  4. movement_rate (churn v2) falls with age by construction. Use the windowed rate for any comparison across ages or characteristics (section 5).
  5. Set 1b’s different includes new vehicles, about 30% of it in a typical year. Use Set 1d’s in_moved and out_moved for moves.
  6. Sets 2 part 2, 3 and 6 are about a 2017 cohort (36.1% to 51.0% of all vehicles, depending on the table). They say nothing about vehicles that arrived afterwards.
  7. Unknown characteristics are handled two ways. Wide tables put them in an NA column; per-category tables drop them. Totals between the two differ by exactly that amount.
  8. Averages skip missing values rather than treating them as zero. An average mileage is over vehicles with a recorded mileage; divide the count columns yourself if you want a per-vehicle figure over everybody.
  9. Northern Ireland is one area, BT. It is included unless exclude_ni_rows is TRUE (currently FALSE). Tables built before October 2026 called it NI.
  10. The row label in Set 1b and 1c is the later year’s area, not the earlier one, so the same vehicle can appear under different areas in different columns of one row-set.
  11. Most Euro statuses are assumed, not recorded. A missing Euro status is filled from the date the vehicle was made, so the Set 7 Euro check compares a date-based category with a largely date-based standard.
  12. The fuel groups now separate plug-in hybrids. Plug-in Hybrid Electric is a sixth fuel group, taken from the DfT licensing statistics for the vehicle’s model (methodology.md, Section 5.5). It is a floor: a plug-in hybrid of a model DfT sells both ways, and whose model text did not settle it, still counts as Hybrid Electric. In churn v2 mode (churn.fuel_column = fuel_DVSA) the group is always empty, because the DVSA fuel does not have it.
