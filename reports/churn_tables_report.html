Churn tables: what each one contains and exactly how it was made

NoteGenerated by the pipeline

This appendix is outputs/reports/churn_tables_report.md from the MOT-pipline repository, copied unchanged into the book by scripts/sync-pipeline.R. Paths such as outputs/... refer to that repository. See Appendix D — Maintaining this book.

Generated from reports/churn_tables_report.qmd by the MOT-pipline churn_tables_report target (profile: main). Edit the template, not this file: every number below is recomputed from the data each time the pipeline runs.

1. What this document is for

The churn tables answer the question “do vehicles stay in the same part of the country, and does that differ by age, mileage, fuel or engine size?” There are 71 CSV files in outputs/tables/churn/. This document explains, for each one:

  • which vehicles are counted (and which are left out),
  • which variables were used, how they were grouped, and what was calculated,
  • what every column means,
  • how complete the underlying variables are, and how their values are spread,
  • the traps worth knowing about before quoting a number.

No programming knowledge is assumed. Where a rule is fussy, the fussy version is given rather than a simplified one, because the simplified version is where mistakes come from. Column and file names appear like this.

2. The short version

  • Every table is built from one intermediate file, outputs/intermediate/churn_base.parquet, which has one row per vehicle (79,741,922 vehicles) and one column per year for location and mileage.
  • A vehicle’s location in a year is a postcode area (“LS”, “BS”, “NI”), or one of three words standing in for “no location recorded”: unborn, dead, unknown.
  • Almost every table counts vehicles grouped by postcode area, and splits them either by year or by a characteristic (age, mileage, fuel, engine size, emissions category).
  • The biggest limitation is not missing data in the usual sense: it is that a vehicle is only located in years it had an MOT. In 2019, the best covered year, only 53.3% of vehicles had a known postcode area.

3. Where the data comes from

3.1 The chain of files

The churn tables are the last step of a longer pipeline. Each step writes a file; the next step reads it. Nothing in the churn step goes back to the original data.

Step File What it is
Input annualised_with_ves_4.parquet The vehicle-level annualised MOT table supplied to the project, one row per vehicle, with DVLA (VES) columns attached
Stage 1 outputs/intermediate/01_core.parquet The input narrowed to the columns used, typed, with vehicles outside the scope removed
Stage 2-3 outputs/vehicles_enhanced.parquet Cleaned make/model, harmonised fuel, engine size, Euro status and so on (93,352,241 vehicles)
Churn outputs/intermediate/churn_base.parquet One row per vehicle with the churn measures precomputed (79,741,922 vehicles)
Churn outputs/tables/churn/*.csv The 71 published tables

“Scope” means a vehicle is kept if it has any recorded mileage, or any recorded postcode area, or was born on or after 2005-01-01, or shows DVLA activity (tax, V5C, MOT expiry or first registration) since that date.

4. The one table everything is built from

churn_base.parquet has one row per vehicle. Three kinds of column:

  1. Identifiers and dates copied or derived from the cleaned file.
  2. Year columns: one pca_YYYY (location) and one mileage_YYYY (miles driven that year) per year, 41 columns in all.
  3. Precomputed measures: age, move counts, movement rates, bands, flags. These are computed once here so that every table uses an identical definition. If two tables disagree, it is not because they measured differently.

5. The vocabulary, pedantically

Postcode area (PCA) - the letters at the start of a UK postcode: “BS” for Bristol, “LS” for Leeds. There are about 120 of them. Northern Ireland arrives in this data as the single area NI, not as “BT”.

A real postcode area - any value that is not unborn, dead or unknown. The phrase is used throughout: “vehicles in a real PCA in 2017” means vehicles whose location in 2017 was actually known.

unborn / dead / unknown - filled in where no postcode area was recorded, by these rules, in this order:

  1. unborn if the vehicle’s birth_date year is later than the year in question;
  2. dead if the vehicle’s last MOT year is earlier than the year in question;
  3. unknown otherwise - alive, but not observed.

A vehicle with no dates at all is treated as already born.

Status in a comparison - when a table compares a vehicle’s location in one year against a reference year, the answer is one of four words, tested in this order:

Status Meaning
dead the comparison year says dead
unborn the comparison year says unborn
same a real postcode area, the same as the reference year
different anything else, which includes both a genuinely different area and unknown

The last row is the single most important caveat in this document. A vehicle that simply was not seen in a year is counted as different, not as missing. different therefore means “not demonstrably in the same place”, not “moved”.

A move - a change of postcode area between two consecutive years where both years hold a real postcode area. A vehicle going LS → unknown → BD is recorded as no moves, because neither pair of consecutive years has two real areas. This makes the move counts conservative: real moves across a gap in observation are not counted.

Age - whole years at 1 June of the year in question, calculated as the days from the first use date to 1 June divided by 365.25, rounded down. A vehicle first used in August 2013 is age 3 in 2017, not 4. Ages can be negative (a first use date after the year), which is why the age bands include a <0 band.

Movement rate - moves per year of life, ignoring the first three years (when a car has no MOT and so cannot be located): n_moves_2007_2017 / (age in 2017 - 3). Only defined for vehicles aged 4 or over in 2017. Windowed movement rate divides instead by the number of year pairs where both years hold a real area, so it measures moves per observed year. Where observation is patchy the windowed rate is the fairer one, and it is always the smaller of the two.

Static - a vehicle in a real postcode area in 2017 that was in the same area in 2016 and 2018. A vehicle unknown in 2016 is therefore not static, even if it never moved.

Bands - every band includes its lower bound and excludes its upper bound. Engine size 2000cc falls in 1700-2000; 2001cc falls in >2000. Mileage 12,000 falls in >12k, not 5-12k. Engine size 0 has a band of its own, 0 (electric), because the harmonised engine size is 0 for the fuels with no engine; the lowest size band starts at 1cc and says so.

The NA column - several wide tables have a final column headed NA. That is not an error: it counts vehicles whose characteristic is unknown (usually engine size or mileage). Tables that list one row per category instead drop the unknown category. So a band table and its matching cross-tab can have slightly different totals, and the difference is the unknowns.

Zeros - every combination of area, year and band appears in the wide tables, filled with 0 where no vehicle matched. A 0 means “none”, not “not measured”.

Rounding - counts are exact. Proportions in Sets 2 and 6 are written at full precision. Average mileages in Set 7A are rounded to one decimal place; percentages in Set 0 to two.

6. How complete the variables are

The table in section 4.1 gives the exact missing counts. In words, for the 79,741,922 vehicles:

  • Location and fuel are never missing. Every vehicle has a value in every pca_YYYY column (though 48.1% of 2017’s values are one of the three stand-in words), and every vehicle has a fuel group.
  • Engine size is missing for 5,936 vehicles (0.0%), so the four engine band columns are missing for exactly the same vehicles.
  • Euro status is missing for 3,841,192 vehicles (4.8%). That looks like good coverage, but most of it is an assumption rather than a record: the register holds a Euro status for only a minority of vehicles, and where it holds none the enhanced table fills in the standard that was compulsory for new registrations of that body type when the vehicle was made (methodology.md, Section 5.7). Euro status is used only in the Set 7 check table.
  • Movement rate is missing for 29,441,222 vehicles (36.9%) - those aged under 4 in 2017, for whom it is not defined.
  • birth_pca (the area a vehicle first appeared in) is missing for 40,238,653 vehicles (50.5%): vehicles already registered before the data starts never have an unborn-to-area transition to read it from. Set 4’s “born here” columns are only meaningful for recent vehicles.
  • The dates behind the age calculation are never missing in the combined first_use_date, but the individual sources are: DVSA’s own date is missing for 8,689,571 vehicles (10.9%), which is what Set 0 reports on.

9. The tables, one set at a time

Each set below says: the question, the vehicles counted, the variables, the grouping, the arithmetic, and the files.

Set 0 - where the first use date came from

  • Question. How much of the age calculation rests on DVSA’s own date?
  • Vehicles. All 79,741,922.
  • Variables. firstUsedDate_DVSA, monthOfFirstRegistration_DVLA, birth_date.
  • Grouping. None. Four counts, one per source, by the first rule that applies: DVSA’s date if present, else DVLA’s first registration, else birth_date, else none.
  • Arithmetic. pct is the count over all vehicles, to two decimals. n_differs_from_v1_birthDate, on the first row only, counts vehicles whose DVSA first use date is not the same day as birth_date - a measure of how much the earlier version of this analysis, which used birth_date, differed.
  • File. set0_first_use_date_provenance.csv.

Set 1 - who is still where they were

  • Question. Of the vehicles in area X in a reference year, how many are in the same area, a different one, dead or not yet born, in each later year?
  • Vehicles. All of them, including those whose reference-year location is dead, unborn or unknown - except in set1a2, which keeps only vehicles in a real area in 2017 (41,356,526 vehicles).
  • Variables. pca_YYYY for every year, test_class, and age for the age4plus variants.
  • Grouping. Row = reference-year postcode area (plus test class). For each year, every vehicle is labelled same, different, dead or unborn against the reference year, and the labels are counted.
  • Arithmetic. Counts only. The table is then pivoted so each status-and-year pair becomes a column, named status_year (for example same_2019), ordered by year then status, with missing combinations as 0.
  • Files and what the reference year is.
    • set1a_...change_from_2017.csv - reference year 2017, all vehicles. Rows include dead, unborn and unknown as reference locations.
    • set1a2_..._cohort_only.csv - the same, restricted to vehicles in a real area in 2017. This is the one to use for “of the 2017 fleet, how many stayed”.
    • set1b_...from_year-N.csv for N = 1, 2, 3 - a rolling comparison: each year against the area N years earlier. The row label is the later year’s area, so a vehicle contributes to different rows in different years.
    • set1c_...from_year-N_age4plus.csv for N = 1, 2 - as 1b, but only vehicles aged 4 or over in the later year, which removes the cars-with-no-MOT-yet effect.
  • Traps. unknown is inside different (section 5). The test_class column is constant (4) because of the class filter; it is kept so the file shape matches the earlier analysis.

Set 2 - counts by characteristic, and who stayed put

Set 2 has two unrelated halves.

Part 1: stock counts by area, year and band.

  • Vehicles. All vehicles that were in a real area in the year being counted; the population therefore changes from year to year.
  • Variables and bands. Age at that year (22 bands, <0 to 20+); mileage that year (27 bands of 1,000 miles); fuel_simple (6 groups); engine size in three different band sets - the detailed one (26 bands), 100cc bands (37 bands) and 4 bands (5 bands).
  • Grouping. Row = year and postcode area. Column = band.
  • Arithmetic. Counts. Absent combinations are filled with 0, and a final NA column holds vehicles whose band is unknown.
  • Files. set2_pca_ageband_vehicle_counts_by_year.csv, set2_pca_mileageband_vehicle_counts_by_year.csv, set2_pca_fuel_type_vehicle_counts_by_year.csv, set2_pca_engine_size_vehicle_counts_by_year.csv, set2_pca_engine_size_100cc_vehicle_counts_by_year.csv, set2_pca_engine_size_4band_vehicle_counts_by_year.csv.

Part 2: the share of a 2017 cohort that stayed put.

  • Vehicles. The 2017 cohort: in a real area in 2017 and aged 4 or over (29,332,613 vehicles, 36.8% of the base). Every “static” and “movement rate” number in Sets 2, 3 and 6 uses this same cohort.
  • Variables. static_2017, and the 2017 values of age band, mileage band, fuel and engine band.
  • Grouping. One row per category, or one row per pair of categories for the cross-tabulations.
  • Arithmetic. n_total is the vehicles in the category; n_static those in the same area in 2016, 2017 and 2018; prop_static is one divided by the other. Vehicles whose category is unknown are excluded here, rather than given an NA column.
  • Files. set2_static_by_ageband.csv, ..._by_mileageband_detail.csv, ..._by_mileageband_3.csv, ..._by_fuel.csv, ..._by_engine_3band.csv, ..._by_engine_4band.csv, ..._by_engine_100cc.csv, plus seven pairwise cross-tabulations as set2_cross_<pair>_long.csv (one row per cell, with counts) and set2_cross_<pair>_wide_prop.csv (a grid of proportions only).
  • Trap. prop_static is sensitive to observation, not just to moving: a vehicle not seen in 2016 cannot be static. Compare like with like (similar ages), or use Set 6’s movement rates instead.

Set 3 - what a 2017 area’s stock is made of

  • Question. For each area, where did its 2017 vehicles come from, and where did they go?
  • Vehicles. In a real area in 2017, all ages (41,356,526 vehicles) - note this is a wider population than Set 2 part 2.
  • Variables. Status in 2016 and in 2018 against 2017; mileage 2017; age 2017; fuel3; engine_band4; movement_rate.
  • Grouping. One row per postcode area.
  • Arithmetic. total_vehicles_2017 is the row’s whole stock. The nine columns from same16_same18 to same16_dead18 are a full 3 × 3 table of 2016 status against 2018 status, so they add up to the total, and residual_other is the total minus those nine (it should be 0; the pipeline prints a warning if any row’s is not). The remaining columns are counts in mileage, fuel, engine and age bands, plus average age, average mileage and average movement rate. arrived_and_left_2016_2018 repeats one of the nine cells under an older name.
  • Files. set3_pca_components_2017.csv, set3_movement_rate_distribution_2017.csv (the overall distribution across 9 rate bands) and set3_movement_rate_distribution_2017_by_pca.csv (the same split by area). Both distribution tables count only vehicles that have a movement rate.
  • Note on the engine columns. n_engine_zero is the no-engine band; n_engine_lt1400 keeps its name from the earlier version but now counts 1-1399cc, with electric vehicles in n_engine_zero instead; n_engine_unknown counts vehicles with no engine size.

Set 4 - electric vehicles by area

  • Question. Where are the EVs, where did they come from, and do they move differently?
  • Vehicles. Two groups, joined on the area: columns beginning ev_2017_ describe EVs in a real area in 2017 (44,582 vehicles); columns beginning ev_2022_, nonev_ or all_ describe every vehicle in a real area in 2022 (41,783,729).
  • Variables. is_ev (fuel exactly Electric - Electric Diesel counts as a hybrid and is excluded), pca_YYYY, birth_pca, ever_moved_pca, mileage_2022, movement_rate_2022.
  • Grouping. One row per postcode area, sorted by 2022 EV count, highest first.
  • Arithmetic. Counts, plus mean_movement_rate_2022 averaged over the n_2022_age4plus vehicles old enough to have a rate. Two groups of columns each split a total exactly: ev_2017_left_by_2022 = ev_2017_moved_pca_by_2022 + ev_2017_dead_or_unknown_2022 and ev_present_2022 = ev_2022_born_here_never_moved + ev_2022_born_here_but_moved + ev_2022_not_born_in_this_pca.
  • File. set4_electric_vehicles_by_pca.csv.
  • Traps. ever_moved_pca looks at every year, so a move after 2022 still keeps a vehicle out of born_here_never_moved. And birth_pca is unknown for 50.5% of vehicles, all of them older ones, so “born here” is only a fair measure for recent EVs.

Set 5 - does moving area change mileage?

  • Question. Do vehicles that arrive in a new area drive more or less than those that stayed?
  • Vehicles. In a real area in 2017 (41,356,526), split into three groups by their 2016 state: stayed_2016_2017 (real area in 2016, same as 2017), arrived_from_other_pca_2016 (real area in 2016, different), and unborn_2016. Vehicles unknown or dead in 2016 fall into none of the three and are dropped.
  • Variables. mileage_2016, mileage_2017, and the 3-band mileage grouping.
  • Grouping. One row per group - three rows in total, not one per area.
  • Arithmetic. Average mileage in each year (over vehicles with a recorded mileage, so the three groups have different denominators), plus counts in the <5k, 5-12k and >12k bands for both years.
  • File. set5_move_vs_mileage.csv.
  • Trap. The arrivals group is younger on average than the stayers, and younger cars drive more. This table does not adjust for that.

Set 6 - movement rates by characteristic

  • Question. The same questions as Set 2 part 2, but measured by how often a vehicle moves rather than by whether it sat still across three years.
  • Vehicles. The same 2017 cohort (29,332,613 vehicles).
  • Variables. movement_rate and movement_rate_window, by age band, mileage band, fuel and engine band.
  • Grouping. One row per category, and seven pairwise cross-tabulations.
  • Arithmetic. n_total is the vehicles in the category; n_with_rate those with a movement rate; the two means are averaged over n_with_rate, not over n_total.
  • Files. set6_moverate_by_*.csv and set6_cross_*_long.csv / set6_cross_*_wide_mean.csv.
  • Which rate to quote. mean_movement_rate divides by years of life after the first three, so patchy observation pushes it down. The windowed version divides by years actually observed. Quote both, or the windowed one.

Set 7 - Clean Air Zone categories and leavers

  • Question. Where are the vehicles a Clean Air Zone would charge, and do they leave?
  • Vehicles. In a real area in 2018 (42,049,969) for the check and the leavers; for the yearly counts, whoever was in a real area in each year from 2018 to 2023.
  • Variables. caz_cat, assigned from fuel and first use year by the first rule that matches: petrol first used before 2006 → petrol_pre2006; diesel first used before 2015 → diesel_pre2015; everything else → other. Plus euro_status, mileage, and the 2018 values of age, mileage, fuel and engine band.
  • Files.
    • set7_check_caz_category_vs_eurostatus.csv - a check, not a result: how the date-based category lines up with the Euro standard, by category and fuel. It is about the proxy’s credibility, not a count of the fleet - and it is now a weak check, because most Euro statuses are themselves worked out from the date the vehicle was made, so the two sides are not independent. To compare against recorded values only, set churn.euro_column to euroStatus_DVLA and rerun.
    • set7a_pca_caz_categories.csv and its long form set7a_pca_caz_category_by_year_long.csv - counts and average mileage by area, category and year, 2018 to 2023. Average mileage is rounded to one decimal place and averaged only over vehicles with a recorded mileage.
    • set7b_leavers_2018_to_2023_by_*.csv - vehicles in a real area in 2018 that are not in that same area in 2023 (26,799,482 vehicles, 63.7% of the 2018 stock), split six ways: by 2018 mileage band, engine band, fuel, age band, CAZ category, and by outcome. In the outcome file the same and unborn columns are present but always 0, because a leaver is by definition not in the same area, and a vehicle in a real area in 2018 cannot be unborn later; the counts are therefore in different and dead.
  • Trap. “Leaver” includes vehicles that were scrapped, and vehicles simply not observed in 2023. The by-outcome file is the one that separates those: dead is scrapped, different is elsewhere or unobserved.

11. How to rebuild this

The churn tables and this document are built by the pipeline, so they always describe the same run:

# everything
targets::tar_make()

# just the churn tables and this document
targets::tar_make(names = tidyselect::starts_with("churn"))

Changing a setting under churn: in config.yml rebuilds the tables that depend on it and then this document. Editing the band definitions or the documentation text means editing R/summary_churn.R, R/report_churn.R or reports/churn_tables_report.qmd - never the generated .md file, which is overwritten on every run.

12. Things to be careful about, in one place

  1. different includes unknown. In Set 1 and in Set 7B’s outcome table, a vehicle that was simply not seen counts as not being in the same place.
  2. Moves are only counted between two observed years. A move across a gap in observation is invisible, so move counts and movement rates are floors, not estimates.
  3. Observation is patchy and age-dependent. A car under three has no MOT, so the newest vehicles are unknown by construction and the last two years of data are incomplete. Any comparison across ages is partly a comparison of how well each age is observed.
  4. Sets 2 part 2, 3 and 6 are about a 2017 cohort (36.8% to 51.9% of all vehicles, depending on the table). They say nothing about vehicles that arrived afterwards.
  5. Unknown characteristics are handled two ways. Wide tables put them in an NA column; per-category tables drop them. Totals between the two differ by exactly that amount.
  6. Averages skip missing values rather than treating them as zero. An average mileage is over vehicles with a recorded mileage; divide the count columns yourself if you want a per-vehicle figure over everybody.
  7. Northern Ireland is one area, NI. It is included unless exclude_ni_rows is TRUE (currently FALSE). It has no BT rows.
  8. The row label in Set 1b and 1c is the later year’s area, not the earlier one, so the same vehicle can appear under different areas in different columns of one row-set.
  9. Most Euro statuses are assumed, not recorded. A missing Euro status is filled from the date the vehicle was made, so the Set 7 Euro check compares a date-based category with a largely date-based standard.
  10. The fuel groups now separate plug-in hybrids. Plug-in Hybrid Electric is a sixth fuel group, taken from the DfT licensing statistics for the vehicle’s model (methodology.md, Section 5.5). It is a floor: a plug-in hybrid of a model DfT sells both ways, and whose model text did not settle it, still counts as Hybrid Electric. In churn v2 mode (churn.fuel_column = fuel_DVSA) the group is always empty, because the DVSA fuel does not have it.
